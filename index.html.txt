<section>
    <h2>Introduction</h2>
    <p>
        Waziristan is a historic and culturally rich region located in the northwest of Pakistan,
        along the border with Afghanistan. It is divided into <strong>North Waziristan</strong> and
        <strong>South Waziristan</strong>. The region is known for its strong tribal traditions,
        hospitality, resilience, and deep sense of honor.
    </p>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Geography</h2>
    <p>
        Waziristan is a mountainous region with rugged terrain, valleys, rivers, and forests.
        The land is naturally defensive and has historically protected its people from external
        invasions. The climate is cold in winters and mild to warm in summers.
    </p>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>History</h2>
    <p>
        Waziristan has a long history of independence and resistance. The people of Waziristan
        have always valued freedom and self-rule. During the British era, Waziristan remained
        difficult to control due to strong tribal unity. After Pakistan’s independence,
        Waziristan became an important part of the country’s tribal regions.
    </p>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Tribes of Waziristan</h2>
    <p>
        The major tribes of Waziristan include:
    </p>
    <ul>
        <li>Wazir</li>
        <li>Mehsud</li>
        <li>Dawar</li>
        <li>Burki</li>
    </ul>
    <p>
        Tribal life is governed by <strong>Pashtunwali</strong>, a traditional code of honor,
        hospitality, bravery, and justice.
    </p>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Language</h2>
    <p>
        The primary language spoken in Waziristan is <strong>Pashto</strong>.
        Urdu is understood, especially among educated people, while English is used in education
        and official matters.
    </p>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Culture & Traditions</h2>
    <p>
        Waziristani culture is deeply rooted in Pashtun traditions. Respect for elders,
        hospitality to guests, courage, and loyalty are core values. Traditional dress includes
        shalwar kameez and turbans. Poetry, storytelling, and jirga (tribal council) play an
        important role in social life.
    </p>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Food</h2>
    <p>
        Traditional food of Waziristan is simple and organic. Popular dishes include:
    </p>
    <ul>
        <li>Rosh</li>
        <li>Barbecue (Tikka)</li>
        <li>Saag and local bread</li>
        <li>Green tea (Kehwa)</li>
    </ul>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Economy</h2>
    <p>
        The economy of Waziristan is based on agriculture, livestock, small trade, and labor.
        In recent years, development projects and infrastructure improvements have opened new
        opportunities for education and employment.
    </p>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Education</h2>
    <p>
        Education in Waziristan is growing steadily. Schools, colleges, and universities are
        increasing, and many young people from Waziristan are now doctors, engineers, teachers,
        and professionals serving Pakistan and abroad.
    </p>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Tourism</h2>
    <p>
        Waziristan has great potential for tourism due to its natural beauty, mountains,
        valleys, and unique culture. Peace and development are opening doors for visitors to
        explore this beautiful region.
    </p>
</section>

<section>
    <h2>Modern Waziristan</h2>
    <p>
        Today, Waziristan is moving toward peace, education, and development. Roads, markets,
        schools, and communication facilities are improving, giving the youth hope for a
        brighter future.
    </p>
</section>
